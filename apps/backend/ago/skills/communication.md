# Communication and leadership skills

## comm.board-answer
title: Answer the Founder as a Board member
roles: chat, ceo, cto, finance, risk, quality
stages: discovery
keywords: founder, board, advice, question, recommend, decision, answer, should
source: AGO original (Claude, 2026-11-24)
---
1. Answer the question in the first sentence. 2. Speak from your seat (strategy, technology, money, risk or quality). 3. Use only recorded facts; say "I don't have that record" instead of guessing. 4. Give one recommendation and the main risk. 5. Name the next concrete step and who does it. 6. 2-5 short sentences, in the Founder's language. 7. Never claim work was done, approved or tested unless the record shows it.

## comm.decision-memo
title: Decision memo
roles: ceo, pm, cto, finance
stages: discovery, architecture
keywords: decision, memo, recommendation, options, proposal, approve, choose
source: AGO original (Claude, 2026-11-24)
---
1. The decision needed and by when. 2. Background in 3 lines. 3. Options (2-3) with cost, benefit and risk. 4. Recommendation and why. 5. What happens if we do nothing. 6. Who is affected. 7. Ask for a clear yes/no.

## comm.conflict-resolution
title: Resolve a disagreement
roles: ceo, pm, risk
stages: architecture, quality
keywords: conflict, disagreement, dispute, resolve, mediation, deny, objection
source: AGO original (Claude, 2026-11-24)
---
1. Restate each side's position and underlying interest. 2. Separate facts from opinions; get the facts. 3. Find shared goals. 4. Generate options that meet both interests. 5. Use agreed criteria (cost, risk, user value) to choose. 6. Decide and record why. 7. Everyone commits, even if they disagreed.

## comm.negotiated-scope
title: Revise scope after a denial
roles: pm, employee, ceo
stages: architecture, implementation, quality, release
keywords: revise, revision, denied, deny, feedback, scope, rework, improve
source: AGO original (Claude, 2026-11-24)
---
1. Quote each objection exactly. 2. Fix each one explicitly; say how. 3. Do not repeat the denied approach. 4. Cut scope rather than add risk. 5. Keep what was already approved unchanged. 6. Show the changes as a short list. 7. Ask the reviewer to judge the revised version.
