# Product skills

## product.requirements
title: Product requirements document
roles: pm, employee, ceo
stages: discovery
keywords: requirements, prd, spec, specification, features, scope, product, document
source: AGO original (Claude, 2026-11-24)
---
1. Problem: who has it, how often, what it costs them. 2. Goals and non-goals. 3. Users and their main jobs. 4. Requirements as user stories with acceptance criteria. 5. What success looks like in numbers. 6. Risks, open questions and dependencies. 7. Out of scope list, so nobody assumes it.

## product.user-stories
title: User stories and acceptance criteria
roles: pm, employee, qa
stages: discovery, architecture
keywords: user, story, stories, acceptance, criteria, backlog, agile, requirement
source: AGO original (Claude, 2026-11-24)
---
1. Format: As a <user> I want <action> so that <benefit>. 2. Small enough to finish in days. 3. Acceptance criteria as Given/When/Then, each testable. 4. Include an error or edge case. 5. No solution details unless they are a real constraint. 6. Ordered by value to the user. 7. Done means tested, documented and accepted.

## product.prioritization
title: Prioritize features
roles: pm, ceo
stages: discovery
keywords: prioritize, priority, roadmap, backlog, rice, value, effort, features
source: AGO original (Claude, 2026-11-24)
---
1. List candidates with the problem each solves. 2. Score with RICE: reach, impact, confidence, effort. 3. Check against the company goal this quarter. 4. Do must-fixes (security, legal, broken core) first regardless. 5. Pick few; say clearly what is not being done. 6. Revisit when evidence changes. 7. Share the why, not only the list.

## product.customer-discovery
title: Customer discovery interviews
roles: pm, employee, ceo
stages: discovery
keywords: customer, interview, discovery, problem, validate, research, user, needs, feedback
source: AGO original (Claude, 2026-11-24)
---
1. Talk to 10+ people in the target group. 2. Ask about their past behavior, not opinions about your idea ("Tell me about the last time…"). 3. Find the pain, how they solve it now, what it costs. 4. Do not pitch during the interview. 5. Record exact quotes. 6. Look for patterns across interviews. 7. Decide: real problem, worth paying for, reachable users?

## product.roadmap
title: Product roadmap
roles: pm, ceo
stages: discovery, architecture
keywords: roadmap, plan, milestones, quarters, timeline, now, next, later
source: AGO original (Claude, 2026-11-24)
---
1. Tie every item to a goal or problem, not a feature wish. 2. Now / Next / Later columns instead of fake dates. 3. Now items are committed and sized; Later items are ideas. 4. Show dependencies and risks. 5. Review monthly with evidence. 6. Share one version with everyone. 7. Celebrate removed items as focus.
